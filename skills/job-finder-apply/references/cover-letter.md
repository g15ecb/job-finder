# Cover letter

Write one only when the listing has a cover letter, personal statement, or short "why this role / why us" field. Optional still counts. The evidence quote belongs in `listing.md`.

Target 160–220 words. The script's `word-count` check is the gate, not a guess. Informal means a person wrote it: contractions, a specific detail from the posting, no throat-clearing. It is still something they can send.

Do not open with "I am writing to express my interest" or "I am excited to apply". Do not call the role an opportunity. Do not use an em dash. Do not invent a story about their product that the posting and the CV do not support.

Use their name if the page names a person. Otherwise start with the first sentence of the letter, not "Dear Hiring Manager".

Structure, then make it sound like them:

1. What you have done that matches the part of the posting they should notice. One concrete fact from `cv/base.md`.
2. Why this listing, using a detail from the page, not a slogan from the company homepage.
3. What you want next, if the profile's interests or target titles make that true. An interest stays an interest: "I have been studying X" is fine. "I led X" is not, unless the CV says so.

Bad:

> I am writing to express my strong interest in the exciting Staff Engineer opportunity at Northwind. I am a results-driven engineer with a proven track record of leveraging cutting-edge technologies to drive impact.

Good:

> I've spent the last four years on a ledger migration and the billing incidents that came with it. The posting's note about splitting settlement off the monolith is the part I want. I've done that split once, and the second time is usually where the awkward balances show up. Happy to talk through how I'd sequence it.

Count the words after you write it. If you are over 220, cut the third sentence of a paragraph before you cut the specific detail. If you are under 160, add a real constraint from the posting, not an adjective.
